Methodology

The rules that decide which organizations the fund holds and how much each one receives. They are approved by the board, applied by an independent allocation committee, and published here in full.

1. Objective

Unity Fund exists to make diversified, well researched giving available to anyone, at any contribution size. The portfolio is built to do as much good per dollar as possible across a broad set of human needs, while staying stable enough that contributors can give with confidence year after year.

This methodology governs every decision about what the fund holds and how much each holding receives. The board reviews it every two years. Any change is published at least ninety days before it takes effect.

2. Eligibility

An organization must meet every one of the following conditions before it can be considered.

  • Registered as a 501(c)(3) public charity in the United States, or a foreign organization that has passed an equivalency determination.
  • At least three years of operating history and two consecutive years of audited financial statements.
  • Annual revenue large enough that grants from the fund would not exceed 25 percent of it.
  • No participation in political campaigns, and no requirement that the people it serves take part in religious activity in order to receive help.

Organizations cannot apply, and no organization can pay to be considered. Candidates are nominated by independent researchers, program evaluators and current holdings.

3. Selection standards

Eligible candidates are reviewed by research staff over a period of at least three months, including conversations with leadership, a review of financial statements and, where possible, a site visit. The allocation committee then scores each candidate against four standards.

  1. Evidence of results

    The core program is supported by independent evaluation, or by monitoring data strong enough to show that the work is reaching people and changing outcomes. Programs with randomized or quasi-experimental evidence score highest.

  2. Cost effectiveness

    We estimate the cost of a meaningful result, such as a life saved, a child reading at grade level or a household lifted above the poverty line, and compare it with organizations doing similar work.

  3. Transparency

    Audited financial statements are public, program reporting is regular and specific, and the organization is candid about what has not worked.

  4. Room for more funding

    The organization has a credible plan to put additional money to work within twelve months, rather than adding it to reserves.

4. Weighting

Weights are set in two stages. First, the committee sets a weight for each cause area, considering the scale of need, the strength of evidence available in that area and the value of keeping the portfolio diversified. Second, each organization receives a share of its cause area's weight in proportion to its scores for cost effectiveness and room for more funding.

5. Annual rebalance

The portfolio is reviewed once a year on a fixed calendar, so that contributors and holdings can plan around it. Distributions continue on schedule throughout.

  1. JulyNominations open to independent researchers, program evaluators and current holdings.
  2. September to NovemberResearch staff complete due diligence on each candidate and a full review of every current holding.
  3. DecemberThe allocation committee votes on additions, removals and weights for the coming year.
  4. JanuaryNew weights are published on the portfolio page and apply from the January 15 distribution.
  5. Every quarterThe pool is distributed on January 15, April 15, July 15 and October 15, or the business day before.

6. Monitoring and removal

Every holding reports to us twice a year on spending, program results and any significant changes in leadership or strategy. An organization is removed at the next rebalance, or immediately where the committee judges it necessary, if any of the following occur.

  • Audited financial statements are not delivered within nine months of the end of its fiscal year.
  • Evidence of fraud, serious misconduct or a failure of governance.
  • Program results fall well short of plan for two consecutive years without a credible explanation.
  • Loss of tax-exempt status, or a change in mission that takes it outside its cause area.
  • It no longer has room to use additional funding well.

When a holding is removed between rebalances, its weight is divided among the other holdings in the same cause area in proportion to their existing weights. Every removal is published, with its reason, on the portfolio page.

7. Independence

Selection and weighting decisions are made by an allocation committee of five members appointed by the board for staggered three-year terms. Members bring expertise in public health, economics, program evaluation and nonprofit finance.

No committee member may be employed by, serve on the board of, or have a financial relationship with a current holding or a candidate under review. Members disclose all potential conflicts each year and step aside from any decision where one exists. Committee decisions and vote counts are published in the annual report.
